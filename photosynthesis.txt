Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants, algae, and some bacteria make their own food from sunlight. Inside the leaves, a green pigment called chlorophyll captures light energy and converts it into chemical energy. The plant uses this energy to turn carbon dioxide from the air and water from the soil into glucose, a simple sugar, and oxygen. The oxygen is released into the atmosphere as a waste product, while the glucose is stored as starch or used for growth and respiration. Almost all of the oxygen in the atmosphere comes from photosynthesis. The process takes place inside tiny structures called chloroplasts and is essential to most life on Earth, because it provides both the food and the oxygen that animals need to breathe.
